# MediPath Diagnostics, Executive Summary

An independent review examined how MediPath protects patient results, sample tracking, and hospital connections across its 400 laboratory clients. It found that core protections are generally sound, but several gaps could let one laboratory's data be reached under another's identity, could let a departing administrator's access outlive its intended end, and could let false status updates enter the diagnostic pathway unnoticed. Testing used only synthetic accounts; nothing was exploited by a real attacker. Together, these gaps represent real but manageable exposure, surfacing five months before MediPath's health-data hosting certification renewal.

The committee has three decisions to make. First, approve a remediation budget in the EUR 2.2 to 5.0 million range to close the two highest-priority gaps before renewal: one letting laboratory data cross client boundaries, one letting administrative access persist after it should end. Second, decide whether to fund immediate work on the sample-tracking safeguard, worth EUR 0.9 to 1.6 million in exposure given its link to diagnostic accuracy and hospital trust, or accept that risk for one more quarter while higher-priority items are fixed first. Third, authorize a short, low-cost check, under EUR 25,000, confirming that the existing emergency data-export safeguard, already well designed, is enforced everywhere in production, so it is closed out rather than left for the certification auditors to flag first.

If the committee funds and sequences this work as proposed, MediPath's exposure should fall from the higher end of these ranges toward the lower end within the current fiscal period. Some risk will remain afterward: no remediation program removes every possibility of a future incident, laboratory client departures, or a regulatory inquiry, and a handful of lower-priority items will still need attention once this phase closes. What this program buys is a materially smaller, better-understood, and defensible position heading into the renewal audit, not a promise of zero findings.
