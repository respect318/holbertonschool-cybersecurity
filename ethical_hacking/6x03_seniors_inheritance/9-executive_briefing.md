MediPath Diagnostics, Executive Summary
An independent security review examined how MediPath protects patient results, sample tracking, and hospital connections across its 400 laboratory clients. The review found that MediPath's core protections are generally sound, but several technical weaknesses could let one laboratory's data be seen under another's identity, could let a departing administrator's access outlive its intended end, and could let false status updates enter the diagnostic pathway undetected. None of these were exploited by real attackers during testing; all were demonstrated safely using test accounts. Taken together, they represent real but manageable exposure, arriving five months before MediPath's health-data hosting certification renewal.
The committee has three funding and sequencing decisions to make. First, approve a remediation budget in the EUR 2.2–5.0 million range to close the two highest-priority weaknesses — one enabling cross-laboratory data exposure, one enabling stale administrative access — before the certification renewal, rather than treating them as routine backlog items. Second, decide whether to fund immediate hardening of the sample-status system (EUR 0.9–1.6 million exposure) given its direct link to diagnostic accuracy and hospital trust, or accept the current risk for one additional quarter while the higher-priority items are fixed. Third, authorize a short, low-cost verification exercise (under EUR 25,000) confirming that the existing emergency data-export control, which is already well designed, is enforced everywhere in production — closing the question rather than leaving it open for the auditors to raise first.
If the committee funds and sequences this work as proposed, MediPath's exposure moves from the higher end of these ranges toward the lower end within the current fiscal period. Residual risk will remain: no remediation program removes all possibility of a future incident, laboratory client churn, or regulatory inquiry, and some lower-priority items will still need attention afterward. What this program buys is a materially smaller, better-understood, and defensible risk position heading into the renewal audit — not a guarantee of zero findings.wq
