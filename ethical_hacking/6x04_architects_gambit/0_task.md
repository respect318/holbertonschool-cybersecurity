Elin Solberg's RFP is a deliberate ambiguity. What she has formally requested is a comprehensive cybersecurity health check across Nordstrøm Power Group's five subsidiaries, timed to anchor her three-year strategic plan before a board presentation in ninety days. What she has not done is define the boundaries of that request — and that omission is architectural, not accidental.
Her unstated needs are more telling than the brief itself. Four months into role, inheriting a group with five subsidiaries under five national NIS2 implementations, she is not primarily buying a technical assessment. She is buying a defensible structure for a board conversation she cannot yet frame internally. The retainer signal is also a partner-selection test: she wants to know whether Vanguard reads briefs or just quotes them. A second unstated need is German integration visibility — the recently acquired solar subsidiary is the highest-uncertainty asset in the portfolio and must be surfaced and bounded, not folded into a uniform group assessment.
Before quoting, four ambiguities must be resolved. The IT/OT boundary is unspecified — offshore gas in Norway and grid-connected assets in the Netherlands may fall in scope, and including OT changes methodology, staffing, and pricing materially. Subsidiary coverage depth is undefined: a group-level health check could mean five parallel engagements or one consolidated review. The German subsidiary's prior competitor audit creates overlap and information-sharing constraints that must be negotiated before design begins. Finally, five jurisdictions with different NIS2 transpositions cannot share a single governing law clause without creating unenforceable obligations in at least two countries.
My hypothesis is that Elin structured the RFP broadly because she does not yet know what she needs and is using the brief to identify which firm will tell her. The breadth is a proxy for the question she cannot ask directly: who can help me think this through?
From Henrik's postmortem, I will apply one decision without reservation: requalify before quoting. The clarification questions not sent before signing become the change orders that cannot be charged afterward.
The decision I will not apply is the implicit lesson that ambiguous briefs from large clients should trigger near-refusal. Nordstrøm is not Henrik's French utility. Elin is competent, her pressure is legible, and the ambiguity is strategic rather than dysfunctional. The correct response here is structured engagement, not defensive distance.
