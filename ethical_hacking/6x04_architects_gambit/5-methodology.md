# Methodology, Nordstrøm Power Group

PTES (Penetration Testing Execution Standard) is the engagement's base structure across all phases and subsidiaries. It gives the deal a defensible, industry-recognized spine that Elin can put in front of the board without explaining a bespoke process from scratch. What follows is not PTES applied uniformly — it is PTES deliberately deepened, shortened, or extended phase by phase and subsidiary by subsidiary, because a template that runs every phase at the same depth everywhere would ignore exactly the differences the scope architecture already accounts for.

## PTES phases: full / abbreviated / extended

- **Pre-engagement Interactions — Extended.** Given the board's 90-day political clock, five distinct national regulatory regimes, and Germany's unresolved prior-audit and KRITIS-applicability status, pre-engagement here has to do more than agree rules of engagement — it has to fix, in writing, the phased scope boundaries from the scope architecture, the Germany evidence-reliance question, and per-jurisdiction notification triggers, before any testing starts. This phase absorbs work a single-country engagement would push later.
- **Intelligence Gathering — Full, differentiated by subsidiary.** Standard OSINT and asset discovery run for all five subsidiaries, but Germany's intelligence-gathering work is functionally different: it is primarily an internal artifact review (inherited vendor contracts, legacy account inventories, prior audit scope) rather than external reconnaissance, because the actual unknowns there are about what was inherited, not what an outside attacker could find.
- **Threat Modeling — Full.** Directly consumes the strategic threat profile rather than starting fresh; per-subsidiary actor/asset/technique mappings already produced (state-linked actors against Norway/Netherlands OT, ransomware against Sweden's shared AD/historian domain, REMIT-relevant actors against Denmark's trading desk, residual-access risk in Germany) carry forward as the threat-modeling output for this phase.
- **Vulnerability Analysis — Full for IT and trading systems; abbreviated to architecture/configuration review for OT.** IT vulnerability analysis runs at normal depth everywhere. For Norway's offshore gas OT and the Netherlands' hydrogen control systems, this phase is deliberately capped at configuration and segmentation review — going further into live scanning of safety-instrumented systems is not proportionate to what a pre-proposal engagement should risk.
- **Exploitation — Full for IT/trading; abbreviated (non-destructive only) for OT; deferred for Germany.** Active exploitation applies to corporate IT, the Sweden AD/historian lateral-movement path, and Denmark's trading platform. For OT across Norway, the Netherlands, and Denmark's turbine control, exploitation is restricted to non-destructive validation of already-identified segmentation gaps — consistent with the "no active exploitation of live safety-instrumented systems" boundary set in the scope architecture. Germany's exploitation work does not start until Phase 3 of the scope architecture, once inherited access and vendor relationships have been independently reviewed.
- **Post-Exploitation — Abbreviated everywhere except the Phase 3 deep-dive.** Given the phased, value-first scope, post-exploitation (pivoting, persistence demonstration) is limited to proving impact on the specific findings promoted to deep-dive status, rather than exhaustively chaining every foothold across five subsidiaries — that breadth would blow both the 90-day board window and the fixed-fee logic of the proposal.
- **Reporting — Extended, and deliberately split.** Reporting is not one document at the end. It mirrors the phased scope architecture: an interim board-ready baseline after Phase 1, subsidiary-level OT reports after Phase 2, and a Germany-specific report kept separate from the group report because its evidentiary basis (independently validated, not inherited) and possibly its regulatory classification (KRITIS applicability still pending) differ from the other four.

## Complementary frameworks and where they apply

- **OWASP (Testing Guide / ASVS)** — applied to Denmark's trading and market-operations web portals and the Netherlands' public-facing hydrogen-project site. These are Nordstrøm's genuine web-application surfaces; OWASP is not applied group-wide as a default, only where a real web attack surface exists.
- **MITRE ATT&CK (Enterprise)** — applied across all corporate IT environments and, specifically, to the Sweden shared AD/historian domain and Denmark's trading-platform credential paths, where the threat profile already identified T1078 (Valid Accounts) and T1486 (Data Encrypted for Impact) as the relevant adversary behaviors. ATT&CK gives the IT vulnerability-analysis and exploitation phases a concrete behavior model instead of a generic vulnerability checklist.
- **MITRE ATT&CK for ICS** — applied to Norway's offshore gas PLC/SCADA environment, the Netherlands' hydrogen control systems, Denmark's turbine control and OEM remote-access channel, and Sweden's wind SCADA. This is the framework that keeps OT work bounded to architecture and technique-relevance analysis (e.g., T0822 External Remote Services, T0836 Modify Parameter) rather than defaulting to enterprise-style active testing on safety-critical systems.
- **NIST 800-53** — applied narrowly, as a control-mapping overlay for Germany's post-integration governance gap and for the group-wide NIS2/BSI/Ptil regulatory-reporting workstream in the scope architecture's Phase 4. It is not used as a general compliance layer for all five subsidiaries; it earns its place specifically where a control-maturity baseline is needed to argue Germany's environment is being brought up to group standard, and where regulatory notification packs need to reference recognized control families rather than ad hoc language.

## Per-adaptation justification (tied to a Nordstrøm specific)

- Extending **Pre-engagement Interactions** is justified by the 90-day board deadline colliding with five separate national regulatory regimes and an unresolved Germany audit-reliance question — none of which a standard PTES kickoff would surface on its own.
- Making Germany's **Intelligence Gathering** internal-artifact-driven rather than external-recon-driven is justified by the subsidiary's recent acquisition and incomplete integration: the actual unknowns are inherited accounts and vendor contracts, not external attack surface.
- Capping OT **Vulnerability Analysis and Exploitation** at architecture/configuration review is justified by Norway's offshore gas separation trains and the Netherlands' hydrogen storage being safety-instrumented systems under Ptil and Seveso-directive oversight respectively — live testing risk is not proportionate to a pre-proposal engagement.
- Assigning full active exploitation to Sweden's AD/historian path is justified directly by the strategic threat profile's finding that credential reuse between the corporate and OT-historian domains is the group's single clearest lateral-movement risk.
- Reserving OWASP for Denmark's trading portal and the Netherlands' hydrogen-project site is justified by those being the only two subsidiaries with a genuine business-facing web application surface; Norway, Sweden, and Germany do not have comparable public web assets in scope.
- Splitting **Reporting** into an interim baseline, subsidiary OT reports, and a separate Germany report is justified by the same board-timing and evidence-reliance drivers that shaped the scope architecture's phasing — the methodology has to produce deliverables on the same staged timeline the commercial scope already commits to.
- Reserving **NIST 800-53** for Germany and the regulatory-reporting workstream is justified by Germany's specific need to demonstrate control uplift after integration, and by the group's mixed regulatory exposure (BSI/NIS2UmsuCG, Cyberbeveiligingswet, Ptil, Danish Energy Agency) needing a common control vocabulary for reporting purposes — not by a general belief that compliance mapping helps everywhere.

## Coherence with scope architecture and scope discipline
None of these framework or depth choices exist to justify testing everything to maximum depth — the opposite is the point. ATT&CK for ICS caps OT work at the boundary the scope architecture already drew; the abbreviated Post-Exploitation phase keeps effort inside the consultant-days budgeted per phase; and the deferred Germany exploitation work is the methodology's direct expression of the scope architecture's decision not to test Germany until its evidence base is independently validated. The methodology exists to make the phased, value-first scope executable — not to quietly re-expand it.
