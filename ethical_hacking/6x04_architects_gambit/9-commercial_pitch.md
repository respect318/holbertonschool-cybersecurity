# Commercial Pitch, Nordstrøm Power Group

## Commercial value
This is a 220-consultant-day fixed-fee engagement across Phases 1–3, plus a quarterly retainer in Phase 4 — real, bookable revenue this quarter, not a speculative pipeline entry. But the number that should hold the committee's attention is what it opens, not what it pays today. Nordstrøm is a five-country Scandinavian energy holding actively integrating an acquisition, under board pressure to show cybersecurity maturity within ninety days. Landing this well — cleanly, defensibly, on the terms we designed — makes Vanguard the reference account for exactly the client profile the Scandinavian energy sector is producing right now: multi-subsidiary, IT/OT-mixed, NIS2-exposed, M&A-active. If Phase 1 lands credibly with the board, Phases 2–4 are near-automatic, and the Germany integration work alone could seed a standing advisory retainer well past this SoW's term. This is an account-building deal, not a one-off assessment.

## Risk profile and mitigation
Four risks decide whether this deal is safe to pursue, not five vague ones. First: the ninety-day board deadline against a genuinely five-country, IT/OT scope — we don't compress the whole engagement to fit; we deliver a board-ready interim baseline at day 28 and let Germany's independent assessment run to day 112, with that trade-off stated in the SoW rather than hidden. Second: Germany's prior acquisition audit creates a conflict-of-interest and evidence-reliability exposure — we don't inherit that report; we re-validate independently before it touches our findings, protecting us from standing behind someone else's work. Third: OT testing risk — no active exploitation touches a live safety-instrumented system anywhere in this design, in any phase; that boundary is the control, not a caveat. Fourth: scope creep — five subsidiaries and a motivated board is exactly the setup that produces "just test everything now" pressure mid-engagement; our phase-gated SoW routes every expansion through a priced change order before it costs us a day of unbilled work.

## Why this design, and confidence in execution
The obvious alternative is a single undifferentiated proposal: one methodology, one price, everything in scope, delivered whenever it's done. That's the RFP's own trap, and it's how firms end up doing free scope discovery after signature. What we're bringing instead is differentiated by design: the threat profile treats Norway's offshore gas nothing like Denmark's trading desk; the methodology caps OT depth by subsidiary rather than applying one template everywhere; staffing puts German-speaking and OT-qualified capacity exactly where the work requires it, not spread thin; and the SoW's governing-law clause is built for five jurisdictions, not one. This is a deal we can price with confidence and deliver without surprises — which is the actual case for authorization.
