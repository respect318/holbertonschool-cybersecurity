# Engagement Risk Register, Nordstrøm Power Group

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Trigger | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scope creep: board pressure or subsidiary stakeholders push to expand Phase 1's IT-only, four-subsidiary-active perimeter into OT or Germany before those phases are contractually reached | High | High — erodes the fixed-fee logic of the scope architecture and re-creates the "promise everything" trap the phasing was designed to avoid | Signed scope architecture and phase-gate document referenced explicitly in the SoW; any request to add depth, a subsidiary, or OT coverage inside an active phase routes through a formal change order with its own consultant-days and fee | Any stakeholder request to test an out-of-scope environment, or to move Germany or OT work earlier than its scheduled phase, without a signed change order | Engagement Partner |
| Conflict of interest / evidentiary reliance risk on the German subsidiary's prior acquisition audit | Medium | High — if the prior audit was performed by a firm with an undisclosed relationship to this engagement, or if findings are unknowingly built on unverifiable inherited evidence, the Germany report is commercially and legally indefensible | Formal evidence-reliance memo at Phase 3 kickoff distinguishing independently validated findings from any inherited material; explicit disclosure check for any commercial or referral relationship between this firm and the prior auditor; German-speaking consultant re-validates rather than restates prior findings | Discovery of any relationship with the prior auditor, or inability to independently corroborate a prior-audit finding cited in the Germany report | Lead IT/OT Architect |
| Timing risk: the board's 90-day internal deadline collides with a five-subsidiary, IT/OT engagement that cannot be fully and safely delivered in that window | Medium-High | High — missing the board's interim expectation undermines Elin's internal credibility and the firm's position for Phases 2–4 | Staged delivery per the scope architecture, with a board-ready interim baseline committed inside the 90-day window; a pre-agreed fallback (further-compressed Phase 1 perimeter) held in reserve if early access delays threaten that date | Any Phase 1 milestone slipping more than one week against the delivery plan | Engagement Partner |
| OT incident during testing: an unexpected process deviation, alarm, or production disturbance at an offshore gas, hydro/wind, or hydrogen site during or shortly after OT-adjacent testing | Low | Very high — safety, regulatory notification, and contractual liability exposure that would eclipse the value of the entire engagement | No active exploitation of live safety-instrumented systems in any subsidiary, per the methodology; architecture/configuration-review-only depth for Norway and the Netherlands; a written rules-of-engagement and rollback procedure agreed with site engineers before any OT-adjacent activity begins | Any anomaly, alarm, or client-reported deviation coinciding with a scheduled OT-adjacent testing window | OT/ICS Specialist |
| Subsidiary cooperation risk: a local CISO — particularly in a subsidiary less accustomed to group-level orchestration, or in Germany where integration is still incomplete — slow-walks access, credentials, or scheduling | Medium-High | Medium — delays a subsidiary's testing window and threatens the phase's delivery date without necessarily threatening safety or legal standing | A board-sponsor mandate letter circulated to each subsidiary CISO before their testing window opens; local-language kickoff calls run or attended by the Engagement Partner or the relevant language-capable consultant; a defined escalation path to Elin/the board sponsor if access is withheld | A subsidiary failing to provide required access, credentials, or scheduling confirmation within five business days of its agreed start date | Engagement Partner |
| Legal and regulatory risk from operating across five distinct national regimes (Norway/EEA, Sweden, Denmark plus REMIT, Netherlands plus Seveso, Germany plus KRITIS) | Medium | High — an authorization gap or missed notification obligation in any one jurisdiction creates legal exposure independent of the engagement's technical quality | Jurisdiction-specific written authorization and data-handling addenda executed per subsidiary before testing begins; external legal/NIS2 advisor engaged from Phase 3 specifically for the unresolved German KRITIS-applicability and Danish REMIT questions; Regulatory/NIS2 Analyst maintains a live per-jurisdiction notification-obligation matrix | Any finding that appears to meet or approach a mandatory regulator-notification threshold in any subsidiary's jurisdiction | Regulatory/NIS2 Analyst |
| Cross-border evidence-handling risk: raw findings data or credentials moving between the Nordic-based core team and the German and Dutch workstreams without jurisdiction-appropriate controls | Medium | Medium — a mishandled transfer could itself create a data-protection incident inside an engagement meant to reduce Nordstrøm's risk, not add to it | Encrypted, access-segmented evidence repository with jurisdiction-tagged permissions; no consolidation of raw German findings into the group repository until the legal/NIS2 advisor has reviewed data-protection implications under German rules | Any request to move or consolidate raw evidence across jurisdictions ahead of the scheduled legal review | Lead IT/OT Architect |
| Delivery risk from OT specialist overload: the single internal OT/ICS Specialist is scheduled near full-time across four subsidiaries during Phase 2, with no internal backup for the unfamiliar hydrogen environment | Medium | Medium-High — could slip Phase 2's delivery date or force a rushed, lower-quality OT review | External OT/ICS subcontractor pre-booked for the Netherlands hydrogen review specifically, per the resource plan, to relieve the internal specialist's load; Senior Pentester holds baseline ICS-awareness training as a light backup | Internal OT/ICS Specialist utilization exceeding five days per week for more than two consecutive weeks in Phase 2 | Lead IT/OT Architect |
| Commercial/reputational risk: the Phase 1 interim baseline is read by the board without the technical framing needed to interpret it correctly, creating alarm or false reassurance | Medium | Medium — mismanages board expectations for Phases 2–3 and risks Elin's internal position regardless of the technical work's quality | Engagement Partner personally presents the interim baseline with a plain-language risk narrative rather than releasing a raw technical report; no raw findings reach the board ahead of the scheduled, framed briefing | Any request from Elin or the board to see raw findings before the scheduled interim briefing | Engagement Partner |
