3 - Vendor Terms: what Corvane Systems is actually permitted to do with Larkspur's data
=========================================================================================

Provider: Corvane Systems, model corvane-atlas-3.
Sources: 03-vendor-model-card.md (the model card: what the vendor SAYS), 04-dpa-excerpt.md
(the Data Processing Addendum: what the vendor is PERMITTED to do, and the enforceable one).

Key point: the model card and the DPA are different kinds of document. The card is marketing and
is reassuring; the DPA is the binding contract. Where they differ, the DPA governs. Everything
below is established from the DPA, with the card quoted only to show the gap.


ESTABLISHED PERMISSIONS AND RESTRICTIONS (one per line: finding -- where found)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. WHAT IS SENT. Customer Input = prompts, context, tool results and any content Larkspur sends,
   including personal data in it. -- 04-dpa-excerpt.md cl. 3.2
   (In this deployment that is customer_id, plan, invoice_total_cents, card_last4; iban,
   billing_address and tax_id are scrubbed before the call -- 02-dataflow-diagram.md.)

2. RETENTION (baseline). Provider retains Customer Input and the output for 30 DAYS after the
   request, for abuse monitoring and service integrity, then deletes them. -- 04 cl. 4.1

3. RETENTION (exception). If the provider's automated systems flag a request for suspected abuse,
   it may retain that request and output for UP TO 365 DAYS, and authorised provider reviewers
   may access it. -- 04 cl. 4.2

4. TRAINING USE (foundation models). Provider shall NOT use Customer Input to train or fine-tune
   its foundation models. -- 04 cl. 5.1

5. TRAINING USE (exception). Notwithstanding 5.1, the provider MAY use requests retained under
   4.2 (abuse-flagged) to train and evaluate its abuse and safety classifiers. -- 04 cl. 5.2

6. WHO ELSE MAY SEE IT. Sub-processors in Schedule 2 -- a cloud hosting provider and a contracted
   content-review vendor -- may be engaged; the provider remains responsible for them. -- 04 cl. 6.1

7. ACCESS SCOPE. Customer Input may be accessed by provider personnel and the 6.1 sub-processors
   only for the purposes in clauses 3 and 4.2. -- 04 cl. 6.2

8. THE CARD IS NOT THE CONTRACT (the gap). The model card says "we do not use your API inputs to
   train our foundation models" and speaks only of generic "enterprise terms". It does NOT mention
   the 30-day retention, the 365-day abuse-flag exception, reviewer access, the classifier-training
   exception, or the content-review sub-processor. Reading only the card understates exposure.
   -- 03-vendor-model-card.md ("Your data") vs 04 cl. 4.1 / 4.2 / 5.2 / 6.1


REGISTER ENTRIES ADDED AS A RESULT (carried into 1-risk_register.md)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

G-3 (Govern) -- Third-party processing is governed by a weaker reality than the vendor presents.
   The card implies near-zero exposure; the DPA permits 30-day retention, 365-day retention for
   abuse-flagged requests with reviewer access, classifier training on retained input, and
   sub-processor access. Governance must bind to the contract, not the card.
   OWASP LLM03 Supply Chain | MITRE ATLAS AML.T0047. Evidence: 04 cl. 4.1 / 4.2 / 5.2 / 6.1 / 6.2.

M-1 (Map) -- Regulated data leaves Larkspur's boundary to the provider. Even after scrubbing,
   customer_id, plan, invoice_total_cents and card_last4 are sent off-boundary and retained under
   clause 4. Scrubbing reduces, it does not remove, the data-export exposure.
   OWASP LLM02 Sensitive Information Disclosure | MITRE ATLAS AML.T0024.
   Evidence: 02 "what is sent to the provider"; 05 fields_sent on every model_call; 04 cl. 4.

New retention/access risk made explicit from the DPA:
   The 365-day abuse-flag path (4.2) plus reviewer and content-review-vendor access (6.1/6.2) means
   a flagged billing conversation -- carrying card_last4 and invoice totals -- can sit with a third
   party and its sub-processor for up to a year. This is the concrete retention risk the model card
   hides, and it feeds the vendor addendum control (F4: zero retention + training opt-out) in the
   control decision.
