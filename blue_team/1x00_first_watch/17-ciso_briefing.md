Our hospital's security currently relies on a weak front door with no internal locks. If an attacker gets inside, nothing stops them from taking over the entire facility.

Our biggest danger is that everyday office computers are connected directly to patient life-support machines and medical records. If one receptionist clicks a bad link, criminals can immediately take control of infusion pumps or lock our patient data. This directly threatens patient lives and hospital survival.

To fix this, we need three immediate actions. First, we must require staff to confirm their identity using their phones when logging in from home, costing $5,000 and taking one week. Second, we must buy secure, off-site copies of our records for $14,400 within one month so we can always recover our data. Third, we must isolate medical equipment from general office computers, costing nothing in hardware but requiring one month of staff time.

Fully funding our $120,000 security budget is a small investment compared to the millions we would pay in lawsuits and fines from a single incident. This protects our revenue and ensures continuous care.

If we take no action, a single stolen password will eventually force us to halt all hospital operations.
