# Clinical Downtime Procedures

---

## Epic EHR Downtime Procedures

### Who Declares Downtime

The **Administrator on Call (ext. 7000)** officially declares Epic EHR downtime. The Nursing Supervisor (ext. 7100) notifies all nursing units. **Begin using paper forms immediately** if Epic is unavailable — do not wait for a formal declaration. Notify your charge nurse at once.

### Where to Find Paper Forms

- **ICU:** Red binder labeled "Downtime Forms" at the charge nurse desk. Paper MARs in the locked lower drawer under the charge nurse workstation. Allergy verification cards in the wall pocket next to the medication room door.
- **Emergency Department:** Downtime binder in cabinet 3, top shelf at the ED charge station. Paper triage forms in the triage desk left drawer. Paper MARs in the Pyxis room wall file.
- **Medical-Surgical Unit:** Blue binder rack at the central nursing station. Physician order sheets in the unit clerk desk right cabinet. Paper MARs on the medication room shelf above the barcode scanner docking station.

### Steps During Downtime

1. **Notify your charge nurse immediately** that Epic is unavailable.
2. **Retrieve the downtime binder** from your unit's designated location above.
3. **Use the Paper MAR** for all medication administration. Record patient full name, date of birth, MRN, medication, dose, time, and your initials on every entry.
4. **Use the Allergy Verification Card** before giving any medication. Confirm allergies verbally with the patient or family.
5. **Use the Physician Order Sheet** for all new orders. Physicians must sign. For verbal orders, complete the Verbal Order Read-Back Form and have the physician confirm.
6. **Use the Clinical Assessment Sheet** to document vital signs and assessments at every scheduled interval.
7. **Do not delay urgent care.** Escalate immediately if you cannot verify a medication or order manually.
8. **Keep all paper forms together and secured.** Do not discard any documentation.

### Escalation Path

1. Primary nurse → Charge nurse
2. Charge nurse → Nursing Supervisor (ext. 7100)
3. Nursing Supervisor → Administrator on Call (ext. 7000)
4. Medication concern: nurse → charge nurse → Pharmacist on Duty (ext. 7400) → attending physician
5. Epic status: Clinical Applications Lead (ext. 7300) or IT Incident Commander (ext. 7200)

### After Restoration — Reconciliation

1. **The charge nurse retrieves the Downtime Reconciliation Cover Sheet** from the unit clerk desk and leads reconciliation.
2. **Enter all Paper MAR entries into Epic** in correct time sequence. Do not skip or combine entries.
3. **Enter all physician and verbal orders** written on paper. Confirm each with the ordering physician.
4. **Enter all clinical assessments and vital signs** documented during downtime.
5. **The charge nurse signs the Reconciliation Cover Sheet** confirming all entries are complete.
6. **File all paper forms** in the patient chart or unit binder per unit policy. Do not destroy them.

## Laboratory Information System Downtime Procedures

### Who Declares Downtime

The **Administrator on Call (ext. 7000)** declares a hospital-wide LIS downtime. The Clinical Applications Lead (ext. 7300) confirms LIS status. Lab accessioning staff (ext. 7500) notify the laboratory. **Do not wait for a formal declaration** — begin manual procedures at once if the LIS is clearly unavailable and notify your charge nurse.

### Where to Find Paper Forms

- **Laboratory — Accessioning Desk:** Yellow binder labeled "LIS Downtime." STAT requisition forms in tray 1. Routine requisition forms in tray 2. Specimen rejection log in tray 3. Manual specimen tracking log on the accessioning desk.
- **Emergency Department:** Lab requisition forms in the second drawer of the specimen collection cart.
- **Critical Value Call Log:** Chemistry bench clipboard in the laboratory.

### Steps During Downtime

1. **Notify your charge nurse and lab accessioning (ext. 7500)** that the LIS is unavailable.
2. **Use a STAT Lab Requisition** (tray 1 or ED specimen cart) for urgent orders. Use Routine Lab Requisition forms (tray 2) for non-urgent orders.
3. **Complete every required field:** patient full name, MRN, date of birth, collection date and time, collector initials, test requested, and ordering provider. **Incomplete requisitions will be rejected.**
4. **Label each specimen manually** with the same identifiers. Attach the requisition form securely to the specimen container.
5. **Deliver specimens directly to the accessioning desk.** Do not leave specimens unattended.
6. **Lab accessioning logs each specimen** in the Manual Specimen Tracking Log upon receipt.
7. **For critical values,** the lab technician calls the ordering unit. The receiver performs a **read-back** and states their name. The tech records receiver name, time, and read-back on the Critical Value Call Log.
8. **Rejected specimens** go in the Specimen Rejection Log (tray 3). Notify the ordering clinician at once for recollection.

### Escalation Path

1. STAT order issue: ordering clinician → Lab Accessioning (ext. 7500) → Chemistry Lead (ext. 7510) → Laboratory Medical Director (ext. 7520)
2. Critical value — no answer after 5 minutes: escalate to charge nurse
3. Critical value — no answer after 10 minutes: escalate to Nursing Supervisor (ext. 7100)
4. Hospital-wide issues: Administrator on Call (ext. 7000)

### After Restoration — Reconciliation

1. **Lab accessioning leads reconciliation.** Compare the Manual Specimen Tracking Log against the LIS queue to confirm every downtime specimen is accounted for.
2. **Enter all manual requisitions into the LIS** in collection time order. Do not batch separate orders.
3. **Confirm all verbal results reported during downtime** are entered in the LIS and linked to the correct patient.
4. **The Chemistry Lead (ext. 7510) reviews the Critical Value Call Log** to verify every critical value has a complete entry with receiver name and time.
5. **Notify ordering clinicians** that LIS results are now available and ask them to verify their patients' records.
6. **Retain all paper requisitions and logs** as permanent records. Do not discard any downtime documentation.
