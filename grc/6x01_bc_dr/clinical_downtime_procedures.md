# Clinical Downtime Quick Reference Procedures

## Epic EHR Downtime Procedures

**Declaration and Escalation**
Downtime is officially declared by the **nursing supervisor** or the **administrator on call**. If the electronic health record screen becomes completely unresponsive or displays a connection error for more than five to ten minutes, you must immediately notify the **charge nurse**. Do not attempt to fix the computer yourself. The charge nurse will contact the nursing supervisor to formally initiate these downtime procedures across the entire clinical unit.

**Physical Location of Paper Forms**
All required downtime documentation packets, including the **paper MAR** (Medication Administration Record), physician order sheets, and clinical progress notes, are physically located in the red downtime lockboxes situated directly behind the main desk at every **nursing station**. The physical keys to these lockboxes are securely held by the charge nurse on duty at all times.

**Immediate Clinical Actions**
1. **Secure patient safety first.** Focus entirely on patient care. Do not leave your assigned patients unattended to troubleshoot the computer screen.
2. **Retrieve the downtime packet.** The charge nurse will unlock the red box and distribute the necessary paper charts to the assigned primary nurses.
3. **Verify patient identities and allergies.** You must physically and verbally confirm patient names, dates of birth, and all known **allergies** directly against their physical hospital wristbands before administering any care, food, or medication. **Do not rely on memory or previous shift knowledge.**
4. **Transition to the paper MAR.** Begin documenting all medication administrations, routine vital signs, daily assessments, and intake/output directly onto the provided paper forms using black or blue ink only.
5. **Handle verbal orders with extreme caution.** Use **verbal orders** strictly for immediate life-threatening emergencies. If a physician gives verbal orders during a code response, write the order on the paper chart immediately, read it back aloud for confirmation, and have the physician physically sign it as soon as the immediate emergency concludes.
6. **Communicate clearly during shift changes.** All incoming and outgoing clinical staff must conduct comprehensive, face-to-face bedside handoffs utilizing the current paper charts. Ensure the charge nurse is explicitly aware of any critical patient status changes.

**Post-Downtime Reconciliation**
Once the nursing supervisor officially announces that the electronic system is fully functional and safe to use, clinical staff must meticulously **reconcile** all paper records back into the patient's profile.
1. Retain all physical paper documentation securely at the nursing station.
2. The primary nurse currently assigned to the patient is responsible for manually backfilling and entering all vital signs, clinical notes, and medication administrations from the paper MAR directly into the patient's electronic chart.
3. A secondary clinical staff member must independently verify the accuracy of the data entry.
4. Forward all completed and reconciled paper forms to the Health Information Management department for permanent archiving. **Under no circumstances should you discard or shred these physical forms.**

## Laboratory Information System Downtime Procedures

**Declaration and Escalation**
Laboratory downtime is formally declared by the **nursing supervisor** in strict coordination with the laboratory director or the **administrator on call**. If you are unable to order lab tests or view incoming results electronically for more than ten minutes, alert the **charge nurse** immediately. The charge nurse will initiate the escalation protocols and confirm with the main laboratory department that unit-wide downtime procedures are required.

**Physical Location of Paper Forms**
Blank **paper requisition** forms, critical value communication logs, and emergency downtime specimen labels are stored in the yellow downtime binders located at the primary **nursing station** on each floor. The laboratory also maintains an emergency supply of these backup forms at the central specimen receiving desk.

**Immediate Clinical Actions**
1. **Prioritize emergent testing.** During an outage, the laboratory has severely limited manual processing capacity. You must restrict all new lab orders to strictly **STAT** and absolute medical emergencies. All routine morning labs must be held until the system is restored.
2. **Complete a paper requisition.** For every required emergency test, fill out a manual **paper requisition** form completely and legibly. You must include the patient's full name, medical record number, physical location, ordering physician, and the specific diagnostic test requested.
3. **Label every specimen manually.** Hand-write the patient's identification details directly onto the physical label of the blood tube or collection container. **Never send an unlabeled or partially labeled specimen to the lab.**
4. **Deliver samples physically.** Pneumatic tube systems may be unreliable during certain facility outages. Assign a staff member to physically walk the **specimen** and the attached paper requisition directly to the laboratory receiving window.
5. **Receive and document results via phone.** The laboratory staff will call the unit directly to report completed results. You must clearly document every result on the designated downtime clinical flowsheet.
6. **Strictly enforce read-back protocols.** When the laboratory calls to report a **critical value**, the receiving nurse must immediately write the result down on paper and perform a complete **read-back** of the patient's name, test name, and the critical result to the laboratory technician to confirm complete accuracy before hanging up the phone.

**Post-Downtime Reconciliation**
After the nursing supervisor declares the laboratory system is fully operational, all manual orders and written results must be correctly transferred into the patient's permanent electronic record.
1. Do not throw away any paper requisitions, phone logs, or result sheets.
2. The clinical staff must **reconcile** the paper orders by electronically entering the required tests into the system, specifically marking them as a "Downtime Order - Already Drawn" so they are not repeated.
3. The laboratory staff is strictly responsible for entering the final resulted values into the electronic system to match the historical downtime orders.
4. Once both sides are verified, physically file the paper logs in the patient's binder for medical records collection.
