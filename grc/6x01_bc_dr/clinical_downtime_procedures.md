# Clinical Downtime Quick Reference Procedures

## Epic EHR Downtime Procedures

**Declaration and Escalation**
Downtime is officially declared by the **nursing supervisor** or the **administrator on call**. If patient charts will not open for more than five minutes, you must immediately notify the **charge nurse**. Do not try to fix the issue yourself. The charge nurse will contact the nursing supervisor to formally begin paper charting across the unit. During this period, all hospital staff must maintain strict clinical focus and follow the established paper protocols without deviation to ensure patient safety.

**Physical Location of Paper Forms**
All required downtime documentation packets, including the **paper MAR** (Medication Administration Record), physician order sheets, and clinical progress notes, are physically located in the red downtime lockboxes situated directly behind the main desk at every **nursing station**. The physical keys to these lockboxes are securely held by the charge nurse on duty at all times. If the lockbox is empty, immediately retrieve the backup box from the secondary supply closet.

**Immediate Clinical Actions**
1. **Secure patient safety first.** Focus entirely on direct patient care. Do not leave your assigned patients unattended. Your primary responsibility is continuing uninterrupted medical treatment.
2. **Retrieve the downtime packet.** The charge nurse will unlock the red box and distribute the necessary paper charts to the assigned primary nurses. Ensure you have enough forms for your entire shift.
3. **Verify patient identities and allergies.** You must physically and verbally confirm patient names, dates of birth, and all known **allergies** directly against their physical hospital wristbands before administering any care, food, or medication.
4. **Transition to the paper MAR.** Begin writing all medication administrations, routine vital signs, daily assessments, and intake/output directly onto the provided paper forms using black or blue ink only. Write legibly and include the exact time of administration.
5. **Handle verbal orders with extreme caution.** Use **verbal orders** strictly for immediate life-threatening emergencies. If a physician gives verbal orders during a code response, write the order on the paper chart immediately, read it back aloud for confirmation, and have the physician physically sign it as soon as the emergency concludes.
6. **Communicate clearly during shift changes.** All incoming and outgoing clinical staff must conduct comprehensive, face-to-face bedside handoffs utilizing the current paper charts. Ensure the charge nurse is explicitly aware of any critical patient status changes.

**Post-Downtime Steps**
Once the nursing supervisor announces that normal charting can resume, clinical staff must carefully **reconcile** all paper records.
1. Retain all physical paper documentation securely at the nursing station.
2. The primary nurse currently assigned to the patient is responsible for transcribing all vital signs, clinical notes, and medication administrations from the **paper MAR** into the patient's main chart.
3. A secondary clinical staff member must independently verify the accuracy of the transcribed charting to prevent medical errors.
4. Forward all completed paper forms to the Health Information Management department for permanent archiving. Under no circumstances should you discard or shred these physical forms.

## Laboratory Information System Downtime Procedures

**Declaration and Escalation**
Laboratory downtime is formally declared by the **nursing supervisor** in strict coordination with the laboratory director or the **administrator on call**. If you are unable to order lab tests or view incoming results for more than ten minutes, alert the **charge nurse** immediately. The charge nurse will confirm with the main laboratory department that paper lab ordering is required. Do not delay urgent patient care while waiting for a formal declaration.

**Physical Location of Paper Forms**
Blank **paper requisition** forms, critical value communication logs, and emergency downtime specimen labels are stored in the yellow downtime binders located at the primary **nursing station** on each floor. The laboratory also maintains an emergency supply of these backup forms at the central receiving desk. If your unit runs out of forms, send a staff member to the main laboratory to retrieve additional paper supplies immediately.

**Immediate Clinical Actions**
1. **Prioritize emergent testing.** During this period, the laboratory has severely limited manual processing capacity. You must restrict all new lab orders to strictly **STAT** and absolute medical emergencies. All routine morning labs must be held.
2. **Complete a paper requisition.** For every required emergency test, fill out a manual **paper requisition** form completely and legibly. You must include the patient's full name, medical record number, physical location, ordering physician, and the specific diagnostic test requested.
3. **Label every specimen manually.** Hand-write the patient identification details directly onto the physical label of the blood tube or collection container. Never send an unlabeled or partially labeled **specimen** to the lab.
4. **Deliver samples physically.** Assign a staff member to physically walk the **specimen** and the attached paper requisition directly to the laboratory receiving window. Do not use standard automated transport methods.
5. **Receive and document results via phone.** The laboratory staff will call the unit directly to report completed results. You must clearly document every result on the designated paper clinical flowsheet along with the time of the call.
6. **Strictly enforce read-back protocols.** When the laboratory calls to report a **critical value**, the receiving nurse must immediately write the result down on paper and perform a complete **read-back** of the patient name, test name, and the critical result to the laboratory technician to confirm complete accuracy before hanging up the phone.

**Post-Downtime Steps**
After the nursing supervisor declares normal operations have resumed, all manual orders and written results must be formally recorded.
1. Do not throw away any paper requisitions, phone logs, or result sheets. Keep them organized in the patient's physical folder.
2. The clinical staff must **reconcile** the paper orders by adding the required tests into the main patient chart, specifically marking them as already drawn so they are not repeated.
3. The laboratory staff is strictly responsible for recording the final resulted values into the patient chart to match the historical paper orders.
4. Once both sides are verified, physically file the paper logs in the patient binder for medical records collection.
