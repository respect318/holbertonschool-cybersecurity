# Security Quick Reference: Clinical Staff
<!-- MedDefense Health Systems | Information Security -->
**Version:** 1.0 | **Date:** 2026-06-06 | **Audience:** Nurses, patient care technicians, clinical assistants

---

## Your biggest threats

- **Epic credential phishing.** You get an email saying your Epic account will be suspended before your next shift. The link looks like a login page. It is not. Attackers time these to arrive at 07:00–08:30 when you are already authenticating and thinking about patients. Signal to watch for: the sender address contains "epic" but does not end in `meddefense.org`.

- **Fake vendor support calls during equipment issues.** Someone calls saying they are from the vendor — Epic support, Alaris, Philips — and needs your login to fix an urgent problem. Real vendors do not ask for your credentials over the phone. Signal to watch for: an inbound call requesting a password, PIN, or remote-access approval you did not initiate.

- **Shoulder surfing and unattended workstations.** Clinical workstations in hallways and nursing stations are visible to patients, visitors, and contractors. Leaving a session unlocked — even for two minutes — exposes patient records. Signal to watch for: anyone looking at your screen who is not part of the care team.

---

## What to do right now

- **Lock your workstation every time you step away** — even for a medication pull. Press the lock shortcut or use the badge-tap logout on shared kiosks. Do not rely on the auto-timeout.

- **Check the sender address before clicking any Epic or IT email.** Look at the domain after the `@`, not just the display name. If it does not end in `meddefense.org`, call ext. 4-HELP to verify before you click anything.

- **Save the IT helpdesk number in your phone today:** ext. 4-HELP. When you get an email or call about your Epic account, hang up or close the email and call that number directly. Do not use any contact information in the message itself.

---

## When something looks wrong

**Use the Outlook phishing report button** (flag icon in the toolbar) on any suspicious email — even if you already clicked the link. Then call ext. 4-SECURITY. You will not get in trouble for reporting.

---

## What not to do

- **Do not enter your Epic password through a link in an email.** Navigate to Epic through the desktop shortcut on your workstation, not through any link sent to your inbox or phone.

- **Do not give your password or PIN to a caller, even if they say they are from IT or a vendor.** MedDefense IT and legitimate vendors do not need your password to fix a problem. If they ask, end the call and call ext. 4-HELP to verify.

- **Do not plug unknown USB drives into clinical workstations.** USB devices found in hallways, break rooms, or parking areas can install software that logs your keystrokes. Hand them to IT, do not plug them in.
