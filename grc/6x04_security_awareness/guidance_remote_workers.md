# Security Quick Reference: Remote Workers
<!-- MedDefense Health Systems | Information Security -->
**Version:** 1.0 | **Date:** 2026-06-06 | **Audience:** Remote administrative and billing staff, remote IT support

---

## Your biggest threats

- **MFA and compliance enrollment phishing timed to your remote-access workflow.** Attackers send emails that look like MedDefense IT asking you to re-enroll in MFA or verify your VPN access — exactly the kind of task you expect to receive as a remote worker. The email domain looks close but is not `meddefense.org`. Signal to watch for: any IT compliance email asking you to click a link and enter your current network password — real MFA enrollment does not require your existing password.

- **Unencrypted home WiFi exposing your work session.** If your home router is using a default password, an old WPA protocol, or is shared with devices you do not control, your MedDefense session traffic is visible to anyone on that network — including neighbors and guests. Signal to watch for: you have never changed your router's admin password or reviewed who is connected to your network.

- **Work files moving to personal cloud storage or personal email.** Emailing a document to your personal Gmail "so you can work on it at home" takes ePHI or confidential patient financial data outside MedDefense's security boundary — and outside HIPAA-compliant storage. Signal to watch for: you have opened, copied, or forwarded a work document to a personal account or personal cloud drive in the past 30 days.

---

## What to do right now

- **Connect to the MedDefense VPN before you open any work application — every time, without exception.** This means before you open Outlook, before you open the billing portal, before you access any shared drive. VPN first, then work. If your VPN client is not working, call ext. 4-HELP before accessing work systems on the open internet.

- **Change your home router's admin password and verify it is using WPA2 or WPA3 encryption this week.** Log into your router admin page (typically 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1), update the admin credentials from the default, and check the WiFi security protocol in the wireless settings. If you are unsure how, IT helpdesk at ext. 4-HELP can walk you through it in under 10 minutes.

- **Move any work files out of personal cloud storage today.** If you have MedDefense documents in Google Drive, Dropbox, iCloud, or a personal OneDrive, move them to your MedDefense-provisioned storage and delete the personal copies. If you are unsure where to store something, ask ext. 4-HELP — do not use a personal account as a stopgap.

## When something looks wrong

**Use the Outlook phishing report button** (flag icon in the toolbar) for any suspicious email. For anything else — unexpected login alerts, a strange IT call, a colleague asking you to do something unusual — call ext. 4-SECURITY. You can also email security@meddefense.org. Reporting after clicking still helps.

---

## What not to do

- **Do not access MedDefense systems from public WiFi — coffee shops, airports, hotels — without VPN active.** Public networks are unencrypted and monitored by others on the same network. VPN is not optional in these environments.

- **Do not use your personal laptop or personal phone for work systems even if it is more convenient.** Personal devices are not enrolled in MedDefense endpoint management, are not covered by MedDefense's security tooling, and create data exposure if the device is lost, stolen, or compromised. Use your MedDefense-issued device.

- **Do not let family members use your work device** — not for homework, not for streaming, not for a quick Google search. Personal use on a work device introduces software and browsing history that can compromise your MedDefense credentials.
